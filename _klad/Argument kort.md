A _conclusion_ solely depends on the _argument_ that leads to it. Therefore, you MUST defend or attack the conclusion based on, _and only based on_ that argument. (I leave inconsistency with the knowledge-set that the conclusion should be added to out of scope here.)

An argument consists of presumed knowledge called the _premises_ and the _reasoning_ that must be such that the conclusion is the _logical consequence_ of the premises. The premises are necessary and sufficient, meaning they contain everything that the reasoning needs and nothing more. Everybody makes mistakes with argumentation. If the reasoning is incorrect, the argument is a _formal_ fallacy: the conclusion is not the logical consequence of the premises. All other mistakes are “informal” fallacies. No fallacy leads to a conclusion that is acceptable as new knowledge, let alone knowledge to build on. There is an excellent “list of fallacies” on Wikipedia.
