

To inform laymen, that's most of us in any particular area of expertise, adepts should 
be honest and tell a balanced story. The press can complement this with good dialogue between different experts and impartial moderation. This dialogue should be based on established facts, solid reasoning, and aimed at growing understanding for both participants and the audience. In terms of getting better informed, laymen profit most from good dialogue, but if it isn't good, analytic skills help to distinguish proper arguments from crap.

In reality, even the renowned press, the big boys, present mostly petty, irrelevant or one-sided debate, promoting a narrow set of viewpoints uncritically, and vehemently scrutinizing others. Some experts collaborate in this, exhibiting certainty or consensus where it does not exist scientifically. This makes these experts dubious sources of authority, but appeals to their authority are done nonetheless. Other experts get ridiculed or ignored, or get subjected to ad hominem attacks, often with a straw-men thrown in. 

The result is that rubbish gets laundered as legitimate stuff, analysis is replaced with conclusions, and what's left is cheap debate about irrelevant details or about framed propositions. Making debating tricks like logical fallacies, single narratives and framing the norm, quenches the capacity to think: to properly analyse and throw out twaddle. This makes people susceptible to fake news, propaganda and laundered rubbish alike. What are you going to train students on? And their teachers? 

As we poor souls cannot distinguish propaganda from fake-news, we must be protected from fake news, and from other viewpoints: we can't put those into perspective! Misinformation must be battled, and those who say it's censorship are bad people - perhaps traitors! Our propaganda first.

Now this sounds all very harsh. In some areas the renowned media do an excellent job. Alarmingly, these tend to be areas that do not matter that much and have no impact on the power balance. In war, the truth dies first, and the mass media help it to die. Journalists are kicked out if they deviate from the narrative, even though their stories are often true. They never get to mentor the next generation of journalists, where spinning, framing and playing safe is rewarded.

I am not saying we should not trust the mass media at all. But we have to regain the capacity to analyse, to call out bogus stories, and to sense when information is missing. We must demand from them multiple perspectives and symmetric application of scrutiny to all of them. 

I should not have to speak up against nonsense or propaganda exhibited in renowned media.
